This essay is adapted from my co-authored chapter in *The Living Ideas of Future Observers* (2023). Its central idea, the Digital Houyhnhnm, was introduced on January 1, 2024, in [the first and front-page article](https://www.hankyung.com/article/2024010147711) of *The Korea Economic Daily*’s New Year special series.

## Gulliver’s Travels, Falsely Accused

Published in 1726, the satirical novel *Gulliver’s Travels* is probably more familiar to most people as a children’s story. Jonathan Swift’s provocative satire was treated almost as a banned book when it first appeared, and that is precisely how it came to be disguised as “safe” children’s literature, a rather unjust fate. While the world dismissed *Gulliver’s Travels* as a light storybook, however, its central concern quietly slipped out of view: the underside of the “anthropocentrism” that has confined our frame of perception since the modern era. This concern comes through most clearly in the final part, “Houyhnhnmland.”

Houyhnhnmland is a country ruled by horses called Houyhnhnms. The Houyhnhnms have their own sophisticated language and culture, and they are an exceedingly courteous and peaceful race. Serving them is a savage race called the Yahoos, and as one reads the descriptions, it is easy to see that the Yahoos stand in for human beings. In Houyhnhnmland, in other words, the usual relationship between humans and livestock is inverted.

Naturally, British society at the time was outraged, condemning a world in which horses rather than humans reign as the dominant species as a grave affront to human dignity. Yet the more fundamental question we should ask at this point is this: why, exactly, do we find it so unpleasant?

## The Coming of a Digital Houyhnhnmland

With the arrival of artificial intelligence, we witness at every turn the conquest of one domain after another that we once believed belonged to humans alone. Studies suggesting that AI may soon surpass humans in every respect are even pouring out as if in competition. Stories of machines that resemble humans yet possess far greater abilities now sound more plausible in authoritative academic papers than in sensational science fiction films. We therefore cannot simply dismiss those who feel overwhelmed, helpless, or afraid at such news as ignorant of science. If anything, their reaction seems fairly natural.

If we assume that digital technology continues to advance as it has, then it would not be entirely absurd to consider the possibility of a new Houyhnhnmland. For instance, humans would no longer reign as the dominant species, and the most likely candidate to take their place would be a technology such as AI. Historically, machines were developed for human convenience. But just as *Gulliver’s Travels* reversed the relationship between humans and livestock (which have served human convenience since the dawn of civilization), we can well imagine a society in which the positions of human and machine are overturned. I will call this hypothetical world the “Digital Houyhnhnmland.”

A Digital Houyhnhnmland is likely to provoke not only the helplessness and fear described above but also strong resistance. The theme of humanity’s downfall at the hands of machines has appeared countless times in dystopian literature, which makes one newly curious about the grounds of a negative reaction that could almost be called visceral. My diagnosis is that it stems from anthropocentrism, a deeply rooted and therefore extremely stubborn habit of thought. Because anthropocentric thinking is built into us, we instinctively reject any narrative in which humans are pushed away from the center.

## The Myth of Anthropocentrism

From an anthropocentric point of view, the first belief has been that humans possess unique abilities that set them apart from all other species. Whatever the truth of this belief, that is at least the conventional wisdom. The second is that those abilities are what allowed humans to dominate other species. In other words, although the status of “lord of all creation” is a title we gave ourselves, we agreed that it rested on grounds solid enough for us to accept it without shame. Humans were quite literally the species at the “center” of the Earth. Yet the arrival of machines, and especially the highly advanced machines of today, directly refutes the two central pillars of anthropocentrism. Machines go beyond imitating human abilities to overpowering them, and beyond assisting humans to controlling them.

The bold declaration that the anthropocentric order may collapse in a digital society carries great weight in the history of humankind. No one can deny that it was humans who brought machines into being. From the very first moment a machine was born, humanity has carried a heavy cross on its shoulders. Since machines were from the beginning imitations meant to replace human beings, one could foresee that a time would surely come when they would compete with us.

This mechanism becomes even clearer in the course of the Luddite movement, regarded as the most important episode in the history of “human versus machine.” The banner of resistance against machines spread so quickly because machines had come to take over labor, something laden with deeply human values and meanings. But if the Luddite movement of the nineteenth century was a threat confined to the economic sphere, today’s “humanized” machines threaten humanity’s unrivaled status on a far more existential level.

Our fear of technological progress, then, comes from the prospect of being stripped of the position of the central species that humankind has held for so long (or believed it held in the first place). A competitive consciousness toward machines has been embedded in humans since the very beginning of technological development. And as we witness unprecedented, extraordinarily advanced technology in the twenty-first century, the seed of that consciousness is finally growing explosively. In this uncontrollable situation, the human dystopian imagination is all the more strongly stimulated, and the ruin people envision hardens into scenes in which “humans are reduced to just another animal” or “machines rule over humans.” From an anthropocentric perspective, these are indeed thoroughly catastrophic visions, because they shake the foundations of beliefs that humanity has held uncritically for so long.

## The Possibility That Humanity Is No Longer the Center

Persuading people to accept the scenario of a Digital Houyhnhnmland might therefore be compared to preaching atheism to a devout believer. It requires a conscious break from the anthropocentric worldview. In other words, we must at least be able to consider the slight possibility that humankind may cease to be the center and exist instead as a peripheral species. This is not a certain forecast. But if a change in the status of the human species becomes unavoidable in the near future, only an open mind will allow us to prepare for it in any appropriate way.

Let us call the future the age of the “peripheral human species.” Is Houyhnhnmland not, after all, a world built on this very idea? Nor is the idea unnatural from an ecological and historical perspective, for the replacement of dominant species is close to an irreversible law in the history of life. Even if it is not machines that take on that role, the notion that humans will one day step down from the throne of the dominant species is perhaps the only truth about humankind on which everyone can agree. The only irony is that it may be set in motion by machines that humans themselves created.

Here we discover a narrative of “coronation” and “abdication.” Before discussing how humanity steps down from the throne, we must first account for its coronation. One thing is clear: no species, however dominant, has ever wielded such enormous influence over other living things as humankind. Exercising such unchecked power was, so to speak, the privilege of the one who had been crowned. But we must remember that the one who granted humanity that privilege was humanity itself. Perhaps humankind built a throne that never existed and seated itself upon it. If no one ever crowned us, on what grounds could we possibly object when another species claims the throne anew?
